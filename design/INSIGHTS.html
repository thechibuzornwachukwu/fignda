Fignda insights

Research for the new promise, and what it means for the game. Written 8 Oct 2026. The plan that follows from it is in BUILD_PLAN.md.

People have not lost the ability to pay attention. They have lost the habit of staying with one thing, and most of what they are handed to read is not worth staying with. Fignda can honestly offer the opposite of a feed: a few minutes of slow, careful reading that ends.

The short version

  • The problem is real, but it is a habit problem. Screen attention has shortened and reading for pleasure has fallen for 20 years. Nothing shows the brain itself has changed.
  • People want out. Nearly half of young adults in one US poll wish TikTok had never been invented.
  • We must not promise a cure. "Brain training" claims have failed in the lab and cost one company $2 million. We promise the practice, not a result.
  • Fignda's mechanic already fits. Hidden words beat the skimming habit: you only find them by reading letter by letter.
  • Our own slop risk is the any-topic puzzle. A machine-written paragraph with clumsy sentences contradicts the promise. The answer is a quality bar, not a label: a puzzle that reads badly is not shown.
  • Keep every game element. Rewire what they reward. Points, badges, boards and streaks stay. They should pay for skill and for winnable contests, not for showing up.

2. What we cannot promise

A 2016 review of more than 130 brain training studies found that people get better at the trained game and little else. Lumosity paid $2 million to settle US charges that its claims about school, work and memory had no support. Fignda is in the same position: we can show a player getting better at Fignda. We cannot show it makes anyone smarter or lengthens attention.

3. Why this game fits the problem

Fluent readers do not read letters. They predict the word and move on. Proofreading research shows the same thing from the other side: errors in predictable words are the ones people miss, and people who make more eye fixations and take more time find more. A hidden word across "a most" is invisible to prediction and obvious to slow looking.

So the skill Fignda rewards is not vocabulary or trivia. It is the act of looking closely at text that your brain wants to skim. That is the one thing a feed never asks for.

Daily puzzles that end are already a mass habit. Wordle alone was played 5.3 billion times in 2024. The format works without a feed.

4. The promise

Slow down. Look closer.

One paragraph a day with words hidden in plain sight. It takes about 5 minutes and then it is over. No feed.

The current dare stays as the hook, because it is true and it is fun: "Think you read carefully? You don't." The promise is what the player gets after taking the dare.

Three things the promise commits us to

  1. It ends. After the daily, the app says done and means it. No "one more" loop pushed at the player.
  2. It is worth reading. Every puzzle, whoever or whatever wrote it, passes the same bar before a player sees it: the words are truly hidden, the sentences read naturally, nothing in it is rude or hateful.
  3. It respects you. No guilt in reminders. Missing a day costs nothing you paid for. Nothing sold changes a score.

5. The slop problem at home

Any-topic puzzles went live on 8 Oct 2026. The free model hides words correctly, but its sentences can read like "tape ark" and "dog rapeseed vendor". That is slop by the dictionary's definition, inside a product that promises none.

Decided on 8 Oct 2026: AI is not named in the UI or in any flow, the way Duolingo makes lessons with AI and does not label them. That puts the whole weight on quality.

  • A machine-made puzzle that reads badly is rejected before the player sees it.
  • There is one feature, Make a puzzle, for signed in players. "Any topic" is Make with the system making the choices the player did not make. One box takes nothing at all, a word, a name, an emoji, a topic or a full paragraph. Each puzzle carries a small topic icon from the icon library, or from our own set where the library has nothing fitting.
  • Machine-made and player-made puzzles reach boards and dailies by the same rule: a safety check, enough plays, enough "Good one" votes, no reports.
  • We never claim "written by a person" or "no slop". We cannot say it truthfully, so we do not say it.
  • The privacy page still names the AI providers that receive what a player types. Not labelling the product is a design choice. Hiding where data goes would be a breach of trust.

Can an AI approve puzzles instead of a person?

Mostly, yes. Safety models are built for exactly this list: vulgar, sexual, hateful, racist, violent, self-harm. There are free ones. They are weakest on local slang, on words that are only rude in Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa, and on jokes about a real person the model has never heard of. So the AI is the first gate, a local blocklist is the second, and the real net is players: 3 reports hide a puzzle at once. The owner reads only what was reported.

6. Six principles, applied to Fignda

From the owner's notes on what makes game mechanics work. Each one is checked against research and against the game as it is today. Nothing here removes a game element. Each change says what the element should reward.

1. Points, badges and boards are the scoreboard, not the game

Evidence
Rewards for an activity people already enjoy can lower their interest in it (Deci, Koestner and Ryan). Gamified learning shows a real but small gain across 35 studies.
Fignda today
A good core, with a lot stacked on it: points, 13 badges, 2 kinds of board, streaks, friend streaks, circles, follows, notifications.
Change
Spend the next stretch on the find itself. Paragraphs worth reading. A reveal after the game that shows where each missed word was hiding and how many times you read past it. Harder finds that cross 2 or 3 word joins. A "clean read" for finishing with no wrong picks and no hints.

2. Make competition local and winnable

Evidence
Low ranks on a big board lower motivation. In one study 31% reported negative effects from rank comparison. Strava answered this with Local Legends: a title on one small segment, for consistency, that ordinary people can win.
Fignda today
A global board per puzzle and per day. Circles of up to 50 exist but sit behind a link.
Change
Open on your own crowd: circle first, then people you follow, then everyone. Give every hidden word a small crown: first in your circle to find it today. Group players in weekly tables of about 20 at a similar level. On the global board show "ahead of 62% today" beside the rank.

3. Respect the curve of complexity

Evidence
Past a point, each added feature costs more attention than it gives back. For a product that promises calm, clutter is off-brand as well as a drag.
Fignda today
A new player can meet the daily, the games list, any-topic, make a puzzle, circles, friend streaks, follows, badges, points, rooms and an avatar editor in the first session.
Change
Keep everything, show it in order. First visit: the daily and the games, nothing else. Each element appears when the player has done the thing it scores. One rule from now on: a new feature must replace or fold into an old one.

4. Anticipation over obligation

Evidence
Streaks run on loss aversion and tend to turn from "I want to" into "I have to". The EU's coming Digital Fairness Act names daily streaks that punish a break among the designs it is looking at. Uncertain rewards hold attention more than fixed ones (Fiorillo and Schultz on dopamine and uncertainty).
Fignda today
The daily already has the best anticipation mechanic in the game: you do not know how many words are hidden. Streaks reset on one missed day. A paid streak freeze is on the business list.
Change
Keep the streak, remove the punishment: 1 free rest day a week, earned by playing, never sold. Count "days this month" beside the run so a break does not zero everything. Lean into the unknown: some days hide a long word, some days a rare bonus, and the result says how unusual today was. Reminders talk about the puzzle, never about what you will lose.

5. Use the drive to complete

Evidence
Effort rises as a goal gets close (the goal gradient), and visible progress makes people finish (the endowed progress effect). Apple's activity rings are built on closing an open shape.
Fignda today
Packs show "12 / 30" and a line. The daily hides its total on purpose, so it shows no progress at all until the end.
Change
A ring that closes. On packs it fills as you find. On the daily it stays a mystery until 3 are left, then says "3 left" and shows the gap. A week ring with 7 parts on the games screen. Pack shelves that show 4 of 12 puzzles finished.

6. Reward competence, not attendance

Evidence
Feeling competent is what predicts enjoyment and coming back in leaderboard games, more than the rank itself. Peloton's personal records work because they mark real improvement.
Fignda today
11 of 13 badges are for streak length or lifetime points, which measure showing up. Only "Perfect daily" marks skill.
Change
Keep those badges and add a second family earned by skill: clean read, no-hint perfect, longest word found, a find under 10% of players made, fastest perfect per pack. Add personal records to the profile and to the result screen ("Your fastest clean read"). Show a Sharp eye figure: the share of rare words you caught over your last 14 dailies.

8. Zero budget

Nothing above needs money. It is design, copy and code on the stack already in use. The one cost pressure is machine-made puzzles, which run on free tiers with small daily limits. Players' own puzzles cost nothing and ease it.

9. What to measure

All 4 come from the plays table already in the database.

  • Players who come back 7 days after their first daily.
  • Dailies finished out of dailies started.
  • Share of players in a circle, a room or a friend streak.
  • Median wrong picks per daily over a player's first 14 days. If the promise is true, this falls.

10. Decisions, 8 Oct 2026

  • Any-topic becomes part of Make a puzzle, sign in needed. A player may type a single word, a name or an emoji, and the system chooses the rest. On screen, topics are marked with icons, not emoji.
  • No person approves puzzles. An AI safety check, a blocklist, votes and reports do it.
  • "No slop" does not go on the landing page, and AI is never named in the UI.
  • The name may change to fit the promise. Candidates below.
