Gazecraft insights

The research behind the promise, the game and the screens. Updated 9 Oct 2026. The work that follows from it is in BUILD_PLAN.md.

People have not lost the ability to pay attention. They have lost the habit of staying with one thing. Gazecraft can honestly offer the opposite of a feed: a few minutes of slow, careful reading that ends.

The short version

  • It is a habit problem. Screen attention has shortened and reading for pleasure has fallen for 20 years. Nothing shows the brain itself has changed.
  • People want out. Nearly half of young adults in one US poll wish TikTok had never been invented.
  • Promise the practice, never a result. Brain training claims failed in the lab and cost one company $2 million.
  • The mechanic already fits. Hidden words are only found by reading letter by letter, which is the one thing a feed never asks for.
  • Quality is the answer to slop. AI is never named in the product, so a puzzle that reads badly must never be shown.
  • Keep every game element. Change what each one rewards: skill and winnable contests, not showing up.
  • Look and feel like a game. A dock you can press, a path to travel with levels and leagues, pages that each do one job, and a first minute that is play, not forms.

2. What we can and cannot say

A 2016 review of more than 130 brain training studies found that people get better at the trained game and little else. Lumosity paid $2 million over claims about school, work and memory. We can show a player getting better at Gazecraft. We cannot show it makes anyone smarter.

We can sayWe must not say
5 minutes of slow, careful reading a day.Fixes your attention span. Reverses brain rot.
A puzzle you finish. No feed, no autoplay.Improves memory, focus, grades or IQ.
You found a word only 8% found. Here are your records.Clinically proven. Scientists agree.
Every hidden word is checked by the engine."Written by a person" or "No slop", while any puzzle is machine-made.

3. Why this game fits

Fluent readers do not read letters. They predict the word and move on. Proofreading research shows it from the other side: errors in predictable words are the ones people miss, and people who look longer find more. A word hidden across "a most" is invisible to prediction and obvious to slow looking. The skill is looking, not knowing.

A feedGazecraft
LengthEndlessOne paragraph. It ends.

Daily puzzles that end are already a mass habit: Wordle was played 5.3 billion times in 2024.

4. The promise

Slow down. Look closer.

One paragraph a day with words hidden in plain sight. About 5 minutes, then it is over. No feed.

The dare stays as the hook: "Think you read carefully? You don't." The promise is what the player gets for taking it. It commits us to 3 things:

  1. It ends. After the daily the app says done and means it.
  2. It is worth reading. Every puzzle passes the same bar: the words are truly hidden, the sentences read naturally, nothing in it is rude or hateful.
  3. It respects you. No guilt in reminders. Nothing sold changes a score.

5. Puzzles and AI

  • AI is never named in the UI or any flow, the way Duolingo makes lessons with AI and does not label them. The privacy page still says where typed text goes.
  • One feature, Make a puzzle, for signed in players. The player fills in what they want (nothing, a word, a name, an emoji, a topic, a paragraph) and the system chooses the rest.
  • No person approves puzzles. An AI safety check is the first gate, a local blocklist the second, and players are the net: 3 reports hide a puzzle at once. AI checks are weakest on local slang, on words that are only rude in Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa, and on jokes about real people.
  • One rule for the daily: any puzzle, however it was written, needs the safety check, 20 plays, 80% "Good one" and no reports.
  • Topic icons, not emoji: from the icon library, with our own set for home subjects the library lacks.

The journey

The owner's verdict on 9 Oct 2026: the Games page feels boring. It is a list, and a list gives no sense of going anywhere. Duolingo's home is a path, Strava has segments and local titles, and both use tiers so that the people you are ranked against are near your level.

  • A path, not a list. Chapters by theme, puzzles as stops, the player's avatar standing on the next one. The full list stays one tap away.
  • Stars by skill. 1 for finishing, 2 for finding 80%, 3 for a clean read. This is principle 6 on every stop.
  • Levels. One number that grows with points, with named bands. It answers "am I getting anywhere" at a glance.
  • Leagues. Weekly tables of about 20 with tiers. Top 5 go up, bottom 5 go down. This is principle 2 made concrete: a contest most players can win a place in.
  • The guard rails. Leagues are where games tip into obligation, so no messages about dropping, a way to leave, and rest days apply. The path replaces the list as the first view. It is not one more thing on top.

9. The first minute

The owner's verdict on 9 Oct 2026: onboarding is not great. Today a new player lands on a marketing page, then a list, and meets sign in as a form. The pattern from games that keep players is the opposite: play first, account later, and every question asked for a reason the player can see.

  1. Play in the first 10 seconds. The landing demo is already a real puzzle. Make it the whole first screen on a phone.
  2. Teach by doing. One guided find with a hand, then the player is alone. No tutorial slides.
  3. Ask for the account at a moment of pride, after the first finished puzzle: "Keep this score."
  4. Build the character before the form. Choosing a look is fun. Typing an email is not. The avatar comes first, sign in saves it.
  5. One question per screen, each skippable, with a ring that closes across the steps.
  6. End on today's daily, not on a menu.

The outfit question

Decided by the owner: the player chooses a gender, and we say plainly that it is to find them a good outfit. Framing it as a styling question takes the weight off it.

  • Heading: "We believe you should look good." Subheading, which is the actual question: "Who are we dressing?" 3 choices: A woman, A man, I'd rather not say. The line under them: "So we pick hair and outfits that suit you. Change it any time."
  • "I'd rather not say" is a full answer, not a skip. The player gets the mixed set of styles.
  • It only sets which hair and outfit choices come first. It locks nothing: every style stays open to everyone, as today.
  • It is saved with the avatar, shown to nobody, and never used for ranking, matching or ads. The privacy page says so.
  • This changes a rule in SPEC.md, which today says nothing about gender is asked or saved.
