Gazecraft insights

The research behind the promise and the choices still ahead. Updated 12 Oct 2026. What has been built is in SPEC.md; what is left is in BUILD_PLAN.md.

People have not lost the ability to pay attention. They have lost the habit of staying with one thing. Gazecraft can honestly offer the opposite of a feed: a minute of slow, careful reading that ends, and then another if the player wants it.

The short version

  • It is a habit problem. Screen attention has shortened and reading for pleasure has fallen for 20 years. Nothing shows the brain itself has changed.
  • Promise the practice, never a result. Brain training claims failed in the lab and cost one company $2 million.
  • The mechanic already fits. Hidden words are only found by reading letter by letter, which is the one thing a feed never asks for.
  • A sitting is about a minute. A sentence, or 2 or 3, hiding 3 to 5 words. Short, but never thin.
  • Play first, then ask at the right moment. A wall before the first puzzle loses people. Never asking loses them later, quietly.
  • A sponsor buys reach, finishes and visits. Count everyone, guests included, and count the link.
  • Keep every game element. Change what each one rewards: skill and winnable contests, not showing up.

2. What we can and cannot say

A 2016 review of more than 130 brain training studies found that people get better at the trained game and little else. Lumosity paid $2 million over claims about school, work and memory. We can show a player getting better at Gazecraft. We cannot show it makes anyone smarter.

We can sayWe must not say
5 minutes of slow, careful reading a day.Fixes your attention span. Reverses brain rot.
A puzzle you finish. No feed, no autoplay.Improves memory, focus, grades or IQ.
You found a word only 8% found. Here are your records.Clinically proven. Scientists agree.
Every hidden word is checked by the engine."Written by a person" or "No slop", while any puzzle is machine-made.

3. Why this game fits

Fluent readers do not read letters. They predict the word and move on. Proofreading research shows it from the other side: errors in predictable words are the ones people miss, and people who look longer find more. A word hidden across "a most" is invisible to prediction and obvious to slow looking. The skill is looking, not knowing.

A feedGazecraft
LengthEndlessOne paragraph. It ends.
What starts the next thingAutoplayYou do, tomorrow
What it rewardsMoving on fastStaying and looking
How it endsYou give upDone for today

Daily puzzles that end are already a mass habit: Wordle was played 5.3 billion times in 2024.

4. The promise

Slow down. Look closer.

One paragraph a day with words hidden in plain sight. About 5 minutes, then it is over. No feed.

The dare stays as the hook: "Think you read carefully? You don't." The promise is what the player gets for taking it. It commits us to 3 things:

  1. It ends. After the daily the app says done and means it.
  2. It is worth reading. Every puzzle passes the same bar: the words are truly hidden, the sentences read naturally, nothing in it is rude or hateful.
  3. It respects you. No guilt in reminders. Nothing sold changes a score.

5. How long a sitting is

The owner's rule, 10 Oct 2026: a puzzle can be a sentence, or 2 or 3. In rare cases, longer. The evidence agrees, with one warning.

40 to 47 seconds

How long people stay on one screen before they switch (median 40, average 47). A sitting should end inside about a minute, with the first find in the first seconds.

Measured, in offices
21 to 41 seconds

The length of short videos that are watched to the end most, across several analyses. The shortest clips finish most often but hold people least. The middle wins.

Vendor analyses, they disagree
Shorter was not better

Duolingo tested shorter lessons to see if people would do more of them. They did not, and time spent learning fell.

Company's own account
About 6.7 minutes

Median session in the shortest genre of mobile games. A session is several sittings, so the next one must be one tap away.

Industry data, 2019

So: a sitting is 1 to 3 sentences that hide 3 to 5 words. Not fewer than 3: a sitting must end with something won. The 25 catalogue puzzles cut into 83 sittings this way, 81 of them 3 sentences or fewer. The whole puzzle stays as the hard one, and the daily is not cut.

Not proven: that this generation cannot attend for longer. The same people watch films and play long games. What changed is how quickly something has to earn the next minute.

What the evidence saysFor Gazecraft

Recommended, not built:

  1. Turn on Google sign in first. A 6 digit email code is the hardest step in the flow. One tap removes it.
  2. After a guest's first daily, show their place: "You would be 14th of 60 today." A real rank is a reason. "Keep this score" is not.
  3. At a run of 3 days, say the run lives in this browser only and sign in keeps it anywhere. Information, not guilt.
  4. Everything else stays open. No play limits and no progress taken away. Nothing locks a player out.

7. What a sponsor buys

The advertising industry's own measurement rules for games, and guides for buyers, name the same few numbers: how many people saw it, how many played to the end, and how many went on to the sponsor. No trustworthy benchmark for visits was found.

  • Reach: games started, by everyone. Most players are guests, so a report of accounts alone undersells the game.
  • Finishes: games played to the end, and how many found every word.
  • Visits: the sponsor's link on the result, opened.
  • Shares: each one carries the sponsor's name to people who have not played.

All 4 are in the sponsor report. They are counts from the browser, not checked plays, so they are never used for a score or a rank.

8. Puzzles and AI

  • AI is never named in the UI or any flow, the way Duolingo makes lessons with AI and does not label them. The privacy page still says where typed text goes.
  • One feature, Make a puzzle, for signed in players. The player fills in what they want (nothing, a word, a name, an emoji, a topic, a paragraph) and the system chooses the rest.
  • No person approves puzzles. An AI safety check is the first gate, a local blocklist the second, and players are the net: 3 reports hide a puzzle at once. AI checks are weakest on local slang, on words that are only rude in Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa, and on jokes about real people.
  • One rule for the daily: any puzzle, however it was written, needs the safety check, 20 plays, 80% "Good one" and no reports.
  • Topic icons, not emoji: from the icon library, with our own set for home subjects the library lacks.

9. Six principles for the game

From the owner's notes on game mechanics, checked against research. No element is removed: score, points, all 13 badges, boards, streaks, friend streaks, rooms and avatars stay. Each change says what the element should reward.

PrincipleEvidenceGazecraft todayChange
1. The scoreboard is not the gameRewards for something people already enjoy can lower interest in it. Gamified learning shows a small gain across 35 studies.A good core with a lot stacked on it.Work on the find: a reveal of where each missed word hid, harder finds across 2 or 3 joins, a "clean read".
2. Local and winnableLow ranks on a big board lower motivation. Strava's Local Legends gives ordinary people a title they can win.A global board. Circles sit behind a link.Your circle's board first. A crown for the first in your circle to find each word. Weekly tables of about 20.
3. Less at oncePast a point each feature costs more attention than it returns.A new player can meet a dozen features in one session.Each element appears when the player has done what it scores. New features replace old ones.
4. Anticipation, not obligationStreaks run on loss aversion. The EU's coming Digital Fairness Act looks at streaks that punish a break. 5. The drive to completeEffort rises near a goal, and visible progress makes people finish. Apple's rings close an open shape.Packs show "12 / 30". The daily shows no progress.A ring that closes. The daily says "3 left" near the end. A week ring.

10. What to measure

  • Players who come back 7 days after their first daily.
  • Games played to the end out of games started, per puzzle. Counted for everyone since 10 Oct 2026.
  • Guests who become accounts: new profiles a day against games started a day. This is the number any change to guest mode must move.
  • Sittings in one visit. If short sittings work, this rises and time on the puzzle does not fall.
  • Median wrong picks per daily over a player's first 14 days. If the promise is true, this falls.

11. How a detective works a case

The game says the player is a detective, so a chapter should be worked the way a case is. This section is the research behind that, and what each finding changes. It is a proposal: none of it is built. The screens are in the prototype canvas "Gazecraft case storyline".

Scene, people, records, in that orderPolice procedure: the first job is to protect the scene and record it, then separate and canvass witnesses, then follow up with records and interviews.A chapter is word clue, word clue, number clue, in the same order every time, set nowhere.A chapter opens on a brief, then the scene, then statements, then records. Real cases: the trail the suspect never saw

No suspect leaves clues for the detective. In the cases below the trail was something the suspect did not know existed, did not think mattered, or believed they had destroyed. Each one is a pattern a chapter can use. Only the method is borrowed: the game's cases stay harmless, so nothing from a violent case is retold.

CaseWhat gave them awayThe patternThe game it suggests
Antwerp diamond heist, 2003The gang dumped their rubbish by a road. A man who looked after the land found it the next morning. In it: a receipt in the ringleader's name, and a half-eaten sandwich that gave DNA. A second receipt led police to a shop's camera footage. He was the prime suspect before he walked back in a week later to look innocent.What they threw away. A receipt is a name, a place and a time.Fit the pieces: a torn receipt put back in order. Then check its time against the other records.
Wolfgang Beltracchi, art forger, caught 2010He tested his paints so no modern pigment would show. One tube of white, bought ready made, held traces of titanium white, which is not found in paintings until the 1940s. His picture was dated 1914. A scientist found the trace in a sketch on the back.One thing that could not have been there yet. Care everywhere, and a slip in the place nobody looks.Odd one out, and "ink, not word": the label says one thing and the material says another.
The Great Train Robbery, 1963The gang hid at a farm and wiped it down before leaving. Prints survived on a Monopoly board they had played on with the money, and on a ketchup bottle. A neighbour's suspicion sent police to the farm.The trace left while relaxing. People clean the tools and forget the game.Match the print: one mark against several that are nearly the same.
The Lindbergh ransom, 1932 to 1934 (method only)The ransom was paid in notes whose serial numbers had been written down first. Over 2 years they surfaced one at a time. A petrol station attendant, wary of an unusual note, wrote the car's number plate in its margin. A bank teller traced it.A list made before anything was spent, and a record kept by a stranger for his own reasons.Matching numbers: find the serial numbers from the list among the notes in a till.

What the 4 share. The suspect was careful about the thing itself and careless about what surrounded it: the rubbish, a tube of paint, a board game, the change from a purchase. And in 3 of the 4 the break came from an ordinary person doing an ordinary job, not from the detective. So a chapter can give a step to a caretaker, a shopkeeper or a bank clerk.

The rule this gives the game is in BUILD_PLAN.md, Direction: the 4 kinds of clue. What the cases add to it: a torn letter answers why, and who else knew. A torn receipt answers what was bought and when. A transfer slip answers who paid whom. Hidden words have a reason under this rule only when the text is a message passed in plain sight, never when it is a witness talking. The game already has a figure who teaches that: the one behind the card with the open eye. So each case's hidden-words step can be a message to that figure, and the messages add up across cases.

Methods the cases and the labs suggest. Document examiners recover writing from the sheets under the one written on, by light from the side or by an electrostatic machine, 3 or 4 pages down. A carbon film typewriter ribbon holds every letter that was typed, in a row. Both are a reason for a method the game has or wants: rubbing a pad to bring up what was written above it, and finding words in a run of letters with no spaces. The points on blotters, lemon juice and which way glass falls are common knowledge and were not sourced.

What follows from it. The sets to build and the decisions still open are in BUILD_PLAN.md, section 1 and the owner's list. One thing belongs here because it is about the promise: none of this is a claim about memory, focus or attention. The colour methods come from a test psychologists use, and that must never be said in the game.
