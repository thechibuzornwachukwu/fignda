# Puzzle standard: paragraphs worth reading

For pack writers and for the AI brief. The paragraph is the product. A player reads it slowly, so it has to be worth the time even if no word were hidden in it.

## The rule

Write one true small scene, in plain words, where nothing is bent out of shape to fit a hidden word. If a word cannot be hidden in a sentence that reads the way a person would say it, leave the word out.

## What a good paragraph is

- **A small scene.** One place, one moment, one or two people or things. A kitchen at six, a bus that is late, a boy and his dog at the gate. Something that could have happened.
- **True to life.** Real objects, real habits, things people recognise. No made up wonders. No useless metaphors must be used. No useless never before known words must be used
- **Plain words.** The words a ten year old and a tired adult both know. Short sentences. Active verbs.
- **Not about the topic.** The topic lives in the hidden words, not in the story. A Bible puzzle is not a sermon. A football puzzle is not a match report.
- **60 to 110 words.** Five to eight sentences.
- **Family friendly,** with nothing rude spelled out in the words or across the joins.

## How words hide

- A word hides when its letters run from the end of one real word into the start of the next ("a most" hides AMOS, "Pat omitted" hides ATOM). Spaces and punctuation are ignored.
- Every word in the paragraph is a real, correctly spelled word. Names are fine when they are real names.
- Never cut a word with a space to make a hidden word.
- A hidden word must not also sit in the text as a whole word.
- Do not lean on one trick. If "a" swallowed into the answer hides three words, rewrite two of them.
- 8 to 12 hidden words, 3 to 12 letters. A few that cross two or three joins are better than all crossing one.

## Do and don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "The cr oss stood on the hill." (a word cut in two) | "Dad saw a cross on the church roof." (the word is whole, so find another place to hide it) |
| "Amos amos, a most amos day." (the answer shown plain) | "It was a most odd day." (AMOS hides across "a most") |
| "She did nothing but say that the dog was a good dog and the dog was happy." (clumsy, one thing said three times) | "Ada fed the dog and let it out." |
| "Verily the child did go forth unto the market of fruits." (not how anyone talks) | "The child went to the market for fruit." |
| "Upon the holy seventh day the pious shepherd reflected on salvation." (about the topic, stiff) | "On Sunday Tola walked her goats to the river." (a scene, the topic stays hidden) |
| "A bat, a mat, a rat, a cat and a hat." (a list made to fit) | "The cat sat on the mat while Mum made tea." |

## Check before you hand it in

1. Read it aloud once. If you stumble, a sentence is bent. Fix the sentence, not the reader.
2. Could this have happened in someone's day? If not, make it smaller and truer.
3. Check every hidden word letter by letter, then run it through the engine. The engine decides the count, not you.
4. Would you read this paragraph if there were no puzzle? If not, rewrite it.
