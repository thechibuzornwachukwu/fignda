## The story

Louis and Louise Baker live in the big house up the hill road, 10 minutes' drive from Lantern Row. They collect. On the night of their display gala the house is full: guests, a band, caterers, the cleaning crew, and Mr Scott Lankey, who runs the house's security.

At 9:30 pm every light in the house goes out. So do the cameras, and the doors that lock from the security office. At 9:33 the lights come back. The 1997 Ember award, a bronze flame on a stand, is gone from its glass case in the display room. Nothing else has moved. Mrs Baker sends for the detective, who comes with their pet.

In the display room the glass is empty and clean, but for grey marks where a hand held it. Lifted and set together, the marks make 1 print: the diamond grip of a rubber cleaning glove. The pet knows that grip at once.

A power cut does not choose. Yet this one took the cameras and the locks with the lights, for exactly 3 minutes. At the power box the fuses are whole and the night was still. The main switch has been pulled down and pushed back up, and on it is the same grey diamond grip.

Mr Lankey says he was in his office watching the cameras when a man in a cloak came in, threw a coat over him and shut him in. He got out at 9:44. Asked to describe the man, he says: about 6 feet, broad, the face covered, seen for a moment. Shown that the switch was pulled by hand at 9:30, he admits the screens were dark with everything else. He saw nobody on them. He was afraid to say so.

The cleaners sign in and out at the back door. Their book is in the staff room.
