# Trees

How trees are drawn, in every view. The forms and conditions are `TREES` and `CROWNS` in `src/map/street.ts`; the 3D street (BUILD_PLAN set 1-map) builds each tree from them.

## The rules

- **The crown's shape is the species.** A tree is recognised from afar by its outline, not its leaves. Block in the mass first; suggest the foliage, never draw it leaf by leaf.
- **A crown is clumps, not a ball.** Several rounded clumps, each lit on its upper left and shaded on its lower right, under 1 outline. The uneven edge is what makes it read as standing in the scene.
- **The trunk tapers** and is shaded on the side away from the light. The bark is the species' own.
- **Branches set the silhouette.** A spreading tree forks low into limbs; a dry tree shows its branches through a thin crown; a bare tree is branches only.
- **It stands on its ground** with a shadow thrown away from the sun.
- **Street trees are vase, oval or columnar**, with the lower branches clear. Conifers stay off the street: they block a driver's view.

## The forms

| Form | Species | Shape | Bark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spreading | London plane, oak | Wider than tall, forking low into limbs | Plane: mottled, flaking in pale patches |
| Oval | Lime (linden) | Taller than wide, dense | Grey, plain |
| Round | Apple, orange, small garden trees | As wide as tall, low | Brown |
| Columnar | Lombardy poplar | Narrow and tall, up to 5 times taller than wide | Grey brown |
| Conical | Pine, spruce | Tiers, each wider than the one above, to a point | Short dark trunk |
| Weeping | Willow | A dome with branches hanging in a curtain, almost to the ground. By water | Grey |
| Slender | Birch | Narrow and airy, small clumps | White with black marks |

## The conditions

Healthy, in fruit (apple, orange), holding a nest with its bird, drying (yellowing, thin, branches showing), bare (no leaves at all). In snow, the snow lies on the top of the crown, and along each tier of a conifer.

## On Lantern Row

Planes on the pavement by the bus stop (1 of them drying), the lime outside the library with its nest, an apple, an orange and a birch in the back gardens, a dry tree by the watchman's hut, a row of poplars along the field edge behind the yard, a pine out to the west, a dead tree in the west field.
